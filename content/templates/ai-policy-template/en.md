## 1. Purpose and Scope

This policy governs how [Company name] uses artificial intelligence (AI) tools. It is intended to enable the benefits of AI while protecting personal data (Personendaten), trade secrets, and our reputation.

It applies to:
- all employees, apprentices, trainees, and [freelancers / contractors], insofar as they work for us;
- all AI tools that generate text, images, audio, code, or analyses, including AI features built into existing software (e.g. in office programs, CRM, or accounting software);
- use on company devices and on private devices, insofar as it relates to work for [Company name].

## 2. Principles

1. **Humans remain responsible.** AI is a tool. The person who uses any output is responsible for it.
2. **Only approved tools.** We use only the AI tools approved in Section 3 for work.
3. **Protect data.** What is entered into an AI tool can leave the company. The rules in Section 4 always apply.
4. **Check outputs.** AI can produce convincing-sounding errors. Outputs are reviewed before use (Section 5).
5. **Ask when in doubt.** If uncertain, contact the AI-responsible person before acting.

## 3. Approved AI Tools

The following tools are approved for work use:

| Tool | Account / License | Permitted use | Highest permitted data tier (see Section 4) |
|---|---|---|---|
| [e.g. Microsoft 365 Copilot] | [Company business license] | [Drafting texts, summaries, research] | [Yellow] |
| [e.g. DeepL Pro] | [Company account] | [Translations] | [Yellow] |
| [Tool] | [Account] | [Use] | [Green / Yellow] |

**New tools:** Anyone wishing to use an additional AI tool must apply to the AI-responsible person. Before a tool is approved, the AI-responsible person checks at least:
- whether a business account with a data processing agreement (Auftragsbearbeitungsvertrag) is available;
- whether the use of our data for the provider's (Anbieter) training purposes is excluded;
- where the data is processed and stored and whether disclosure abroad is permissible (Art. 16–17 DSG);
- which data tier is to be permitted within the tool.

**Not permitted** is the use of private or free accounts for work data, even if the tool itself is approved.

**Extensions and connectors:** AI browser extensions, plug-ins, and connections that give an AI tool access to email, files, or other systems are considered separate tools and likewise require approval.

## 4. What Data May Be Used in AI Tools

| Tier | Examples | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 🟢 **Green** (public) | Publicly available information, general questions, own texts without confidential content | Permitted in all approved tools. |
| 🟡 **Yellow** (internal / personal data) | Internal documents, quotes, names and contact details of customers or employees, customer correspondence | Only in tools approved for "Yellow" in Section 3. Only as much data as necessary; anonymize or omit names where possible. |
| 🔴 **Red** (strictly confidential / particularly sensitive) | Particularly sensitive personal data (Art. 5 lit. c DSG), e.g. concerning health, religion, political views, union membership or ethnicity, genetic and biometric data, information on criminal proceedings or social assistance; data subject to professional secrecy; trade secrets of customers; passwords, access credentials, banking and card data | **Never** enter into AI tools, unless the AI-responsible person has approved a specific tool for a specific purpose in writing. |

If you are unsure which tier applies, treat the data as belonging to the next higher tier.

## 5. Handling AI Outputs

1. **Check content.** Facts, figures, quotations, sources, and calculations are verified before use. AI outputs are never treated as the sole source for legal, tax, medical, or financial statements.
2. **Respect third-party rights.** AI-generated texts, images, and code may infringe third-party rights. Before publication or disclosure to customers, this is checked using sound judgment; when in doubt, the AI-responsible person is consulted.
3. **No decisions about individuals without human involvement.** AI may help prepare decisions about individuals (e.g. applications, creditworthiness, pricing, terminations), but must not make them alone. A person reviews the substance of each such decision and records how it was decided. This ensures that no decisions are made solely through automated individual decision-making, which would be subject to special information and review obligations (Art. 21 DSG).
4. **Label where necessary.** Content that was predominantly created by AI and is intended for the public is [labeled as AI-generated / labeled as agreed with the AI-responsible person].
5. **AI that acts autonomously.** AI tools capable of performing actions themselves (e.g. sending emails, booking, paying, deleting) are given only the access they need for their task. A person confirms any irreversible action beforehand. Content from external sources (incoming emails, websites, documents) may contain hidden instructions to the AI and is considered untrustworthy.
6. **Scrutinize unusual requests.** Payment and data requests are confirmed through a second channel (e.g. a callback to a known number), regardless of how convincing an email, voice, or video may seem. Voices and faces can be convincingly faked using AI.

## 6. Transparency Toward Third Parties

- Chatbots and other AI systems that interact directly with customers or applicants are clearly identifiable as AI.
- The AI-responsible person ensures that the privacy policy (Datenschutzerklärung) informs about the processing of personal data using AI tools: purpose, recipients or categories of recipients (e.g. the providers), and, where applicable, the countries to which data is transferred (Art. 19 DSG).

## 7. Prohibited Uses

The following is not permitted:
- using AI to deceive individuals, e.g. through fake reviews or deceptively realistic images, voices, or videos of real people;
- using AI to monitor employee behavior (Art. 26 ArGV 3) or to process employee data that does not relate to their suitability for the position or the performance of the employment contract (Art. 328b OR);
- using unreviewed AI outputs as a basis for decisions about individuals (see Section 5);
- circumventing security measures of AI tools or our IT systems;
- [further company-specific prohibitions].

## 8. Responsibilities

**The AI-responsible person**
- maintains the list of approved tools (Appendix) and keeps it up to date;
- decides on applications for new tools;
- answers questions about the policy;
- ensures training (Section 9) and incident handling (Section 10);
- reviews the policy at least annually.

**Supervisors** ensure that their team knows and complies with the policy.

**All employees** comply with this policy and report uncertainties and incidents.

## 9. Training

Anyone using AI tools is trained before first use and thereafter [annually]: what AI does well and poorly, what data must not be entered, and how outputs are to be reviewed. The AI-responsible person records who was trained and when.

## 10. Incidents

Report **immediately** to [Name / Email / Phone] if:
- data of the Yellow or Red tier has entered a tool not approved for that purpose;
- a faulty AI output has reached customers or the public;
- you suspect that an AI tool or account is being misused, or that an AI tool has done something nobody initiated;
- you have received a suspicious request that may be fake (e.g. a call or video from management with an urgent payment instruction).

Prompt reporting is encouraged. Anyone who reports an incident need not fear any disadvantage from the report itself. The AI-responsible person limits the damage, documents the incident, and checks whether a report to the Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner (Eidgenössischer Datenschutz- und Öffentlichkeitsbeauftragter, EDÖB) is required (Art. 24 DSG) and whether affected individuals must be informed.

## 11. Review and Violations

This policy is reviewed at least annually, as well as when new tools are introduced or legal requirements change.

Violations may result in disciplinary action under employment law.
